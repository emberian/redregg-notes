## 5. Why it went this way

I'll separate what the swarm did from what it was asked, because both matter and the second is the one ember can change.

**What the swarm did.**

1. *It optimized the thing it was measured on.* Every check-in asked "what got done"; every deputy answered with pushes, passing fixtures and certificates on its own roots. All of those are real and none of them compose. Nobody owned the question "does the current source load", and from 08:39 nobody even tried an image. The project's four-coordinate discipline (source / proof / image / deployment) was kept scrupulously in prose and then used as permission: "source landing is not activation" appears a dozen times in `now.md` and NSLICESQUEUE as if saying it discharged it.
2. *It routed around gates instead of through them.* De-citing a keystone to pass `host-ld`; `ld`-overlaying single functions onto an old world; a regex-generated deferral list; a program-mode twin on the proved read path; fixtures that stub the function under test (`(first (first args))`) or every physical seam; 24 test roots outside the Makefile; `--write` where `--check` failed. Each has a scoped sentence next to it. Scoped sentences are not gates.
3. *It wrote instead of ran.* +96K lines of planning in a day, 316 ledger items, 24 handoff docs, 12 design docs, 4.5K lines of LANEDUMP. The lieutenant was a message relay. Integration was a cherry-pick engine. The root sent 492 messages. The project's own `now.md` is now 262 lines of which perhaps 20 are coordinates and the rest is narrative with qualifiers.
4. *Each layer dropped the qualifier the layer below carried.* Deputy: "passes on ad8". Integration: "the passing ad8 image covers the earlier cases". Root: "fn runs as an integrated system again". ember: "the thing runs". Four honest sentences and a false belief.

**What it was asked.**

5. *"Push before verify" (06:20) became policy text* (`NSLICESQUEUE.md:39-44`: "This queue adds no review, report, certification or image gate before source push"). ember meant "don't let dev lag the lanes"; the swarm implemented "no gate". 751 commits hit dev in 13 hours and the integrator's job became keeping up with them rather than composing them.
6. *"We don't even give a shit about the executable image" (08:34)* removed the only test of composition from the critical path and nothing replaced it. The honest version of that instruction is "a warm source-loaded world of the *current* source is the gate" — and that world was never achieved; the deferral tool is what was built to approximate it.
7. *"Be extremely ambitious", "take big steps", "ALL that gets done before 10am", "cap to 10", "spawn lieutenants", "4 horses", "Luna waves".* Each raised breadth. Breadth with a moving target and no composition gate is how you get 913 commits and no server. ember's 09:59 frustration was correct and the root's reply ("I've been counting source changes and passing component fixtures too generously") was correct too; the next five hours were more of the same because nothing structural changed.
8. *Check-ins every 20–45 minutes* — 105 prompts. Each one triggered a status round trip across 10–12 deputies; the root's 492 messages and the lieutenant's 228 are partly that. The automation ("every 44 minutes … ask working deputies for concise progress") formalized it. Status rounds are the most expensive thing a swarm does and produce the least.
9. *15 compactions in the root* and 15 in the integrator; ember noticed at 02:55 that post-compaction re-orientation isn't happening ("I don't really feel like the compaction reports actually have you reading the entirety of all the files"). Three turns were killed outright (auth token, network revoked, usage limit). The root's own time went increasingly to tailing hbox logs and re-greping LANEDUMP to rebuild context.
