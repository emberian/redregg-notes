# How a lane builds (real 2026-10-27, Ember): reason across the whole change; build once

Ember: "too many parallel lanes fighting for the CPUs and all redoing the same proof work constantly. It's something systematic about the way we're using lake and iterating one statement at a time instead of iterating across the whole system and using mental simulation and reasoning to develop without wasteful rerun loops."

Measured: 50 private copies of the build (27 under ~/nd-land, 20 under /tank wt), each seeded from a different stale tree; every rebase onto a moved main rebuilds everything downstream of the change, in every lane, again; the disk at 95%.

THE RULE, effective now, for every lane (Claude, GLM, Astra):

1. **Reason first, build once.** Read the whole reach of your change (the definitions, every theorem that mentions them, every consumer) BEFORE editing. Simulate the change in your head across all of it: which statements break, which proofs need a case, which rows change. Write ALL the edits. Then ONE build of your reach. Read the whole error log. Fix everything it names. Build again. Two builds per landing is the norm; five is a smell; a build per statement is forbidden.
2. **Single-file checks, not tree builds, while iterating.** To check one module you are editing: `scripts/nd-check <that file>` (elaborates it, and the changed modules it imports, against the warm build's oleans; a second run after a fix elaborates only what changed). `scripts/nd-build lake env lean <that file>` does the same only in a tree with a `.lake` of its own, and no lane has one now. The whole reach (`scripts/nd-check` with no arguments) is for the final check only.
3. **No new copies of the build.** Do not `cp -a` a `.lake`; do not run `lake build` in a lane. hbox keeps ONE warm build of origin/main: `~/nd-land/warm`, advanced after every landing by `scripts/warm` (one process, one build slot; `scripts/warm status` prints its commit, its verdict and the published generation). `scripts/nd-check` elaborates your REACH (the modules you changed and every importer) against that build's published generation, in import order, with lake's own invocation, writing only your modules' outputs to `~/nd-land/<lane>/out` (`<worktree>/.lake/nd/out` when the worktree is itself under `~/nd-land`); it links the exes your change reaches there and takes every other exe from the generation, so `out/bin` is a whole bin directory. An unchanged module is never rebuilt and a lane needs no `.lake`. If your HEAD does not contain the generation's commit, nd-check says "rebase" and stops. `scripts/commit` runs the same check on the rebased commit and the rows over `out/bin`. The rule and its critique: decisions/gates.md GATES-32.
4. **Rebase ONCE, at the end.** Do not rebase while iterating. When your change is green: fetch, rebase once, rebuild only what the rebase touched (nd-check does this), push. If main moved again, rebase and push again without rebuilding unless a touched file changed.
5. **Land small, soon.** A landing is one coherent change, not a day's work. Close out what you have; do not start the next card before the current one is on main.
6. **No sub-forks.** A lane does not spawn agents.
7. Everything else in WORK-RULES-20261027.md stands.

8. **A Sonnet or GLM lane stuck in proof engineering yields.** One honest attempt per METHOD §1 (read the reach, write the proof, one build, fix the log, one more build). If a theorem still resists, write the exact state to the report (the statement, the error, the missing lemma, the files touched), land whatever else is green, and stop; an Opus successor takes the proof from the report. Grinding at a proof is never a faerie's or a GLM worker's job.
