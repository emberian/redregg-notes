CRIT-SPEC — 2026-10-27 — outside statement review by Codex.

The landed Spec layer does not establish the advertised correctness of a verified single-writer World. Its nine predicates leave member intent, authority confinement, crash survival, contextual execution, and the authority of foreign heads outside the apex. Several predicates also admit countermodels that violate their own prose descriptions.

I read those documents locally and read the repository through ssh. This is a source-level statement review. I ran no builds and made no repository changes. The countermodels below are logical constructions from the predicates, not reports of exploits run against the binaries.

The readers' demand for one reviewed statement identifies a real specification boundary. Their description of that boundary is too compressed. CompCert's theorem relates assembly observations to source-language observations through separately defined semantics. [CompCert's whole-program preservation statements](https://compcert.org/doc/html/compcert.driver.Complements.html) expose that relation directly. seL4's security claims depend on access-control policy and configuration assumptions. [seL4's proof account](https://sel4.systems/Verification/proofs) distinguishes those claims from functional refinement. IronFleet connects implementations to concise safety specifications through refinement. [IronFleet's paper](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/ironfleet-proving-practical-distributed-systems-correct/) makes that connection the object of verification. None of these boundaries becomes small merely because a theorem has a short name. Every definition that determines the theorem's meaning remains part of the semantic review.

OneHistory.member is a recoverability-to-some-start property. A member needs continuity from the particular start it authorized. Start.genesis accepts every application of genesisWorld. It contains no equality tying that World's origin to me.world. It contains no evidence that the member chose the genesis inputs. It contains no checkGenesis premise. Kernel/Receive/Follow.lean explicitly describes genesisWorld as unchecked. The conclusion then existentially chooses a fresh start w₁. It does not require w₁ to be the original w₀. It does not require a replacement start to correspond to an adoption event in the execution.

A follower that replaces its World with a fixed unrelated genesis after every frame satisfies A1. Its witness is that genesis followed by the empty log. It can erase an entire accepted history this way. Keep every other System field deployed and have this follower return a non-ok result on every frame. All nine listed safety predicates still hold. The two success predicates have no successful follow step to constrain. The other predicates do not constrain this replacement of follow. This countermodel requires no hash collision, forged signature, or malicious sponsor. It is a defect in the statement's continuity guarantee.

The deployed proof uses stronger facts than A1 retains. Command.step permits adoption only from atGenesis. Its adoption arm checks the resulting origin against me.world. Its current code also calls Head.certifiesWorld. Start and the A1 conclusion discard the adoption guard and the certificate evidence. A theorem over the actual execution should retain the member's authorized start and every authorized change of trust anchor. It should preserve the accepted prefix between those changes. A bag of permissible endpoint witnesses cannot express that obligation.

The keyFaithful premise does substantive security work. It excludes continuations that substitute an untrusted sponsor key. The deployment cannot discharge that premise inside the reviewed theorem because the continuation is externally supplied bytes. The premise also says nothing about whether the member requested the adoption. A relay can name the right sponsor key while inventing a command. Start.pinned establishes a signature check and a digest match. It does not establish the sponsor's truthful description of the pre-pin history. A1 is also proved for every base-digest function d. That generality includes a constant function. The formal statement therefore promises digest agreement, with the binding interpretation supplied separately by the deployed digest's security assumption.

A1's floor tests a conspicuously malformed World. told raises height above steps and changes origin. Its refutation uses those facts. A follower that enforces those structural invariants while accepting an unauthorized but structurally valid history is a different adversary. The reset-to-genesis countermodel passes those structural invariants. The design's proposed non-owner-write floor addressed re-execution more directly than the landed told floor. inhabited_audited establishes a re-derived rotate. It does not exhibit a Reach execution that absorbs the rotate from the member's chosen start. It therefore does not inhabit A1's deployed protocol behavior.

OneHistory.pair is conditional head binding. It assumes both equal step counts and equal chains. A writer that sends two valid divergent logs with different chains satisfies it. The members can remain isolated on those forks forever. The common starting World g is another substantive premise. A1's existentially chosen starts do not supply that common g. A2's conclusion states equality of final Worlds. It does not itself state equality of the two logs or preservation of every accepted prefix.

The pair claim belongs below a protocol-level consistency theorem. SUNDR's guarantee concerns client histories under a malicious server. Its detection condition requires clients to encounter one another's modifications. [The SUNDR paper's account](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi-04/secure-untrusted-data-repository-sundr) makes that condition explicit. Nanodregg now has sync anchors, member attestations, self-head posts, and checks against retained past chains. The older verdict's assertion that members never compare heads is stale. The missing top-level result is a theorem over Command.step and the Store actions that execute those comparisons. It must cover unequal positions, anchors already passed, forgotten positions, and adopted bases. Missing retained history must produce an explicit absence of evidence. It cannot count as successful consistency verification. A mismatch between member attestations also does not identify a dishonest writer without evidence attributable to that writer or the configured authority.

A2's chainless floor tests whether the chain binds changes. It is the right mutation for that lemma. It is no test of fork consistency. The explicit CollidesAmong alternative identifies the exceptional cryptographic event. Excluding that alternative in a deployment is a cryptographic assumption. It is not a consequence of the finite witness run having no collision.

Integrity.value states aggregate conservation. A holder also needs authorization of debits, correct recipients, local balance constraints, and enforcement of delegated spending limits. A kernel that transfers a victim's balance to an attacker can preserve the sum exactly. A kernel that increases one account's permitted debt while compensating another account's floor can preserve the floor sum. Neither behavior is excluded by the aggregate equalities. Grant caps and escrow obligations are absent from this predicate. Other kernel lemmas do not become top-level guarantees merely because they exist.

The w.Canon premise makes the aggregate representation meaningful. The generic claim legitimately takes that premise. The advertised apex still needs to establish it for the actual member start. Start.pinned does not carry it. A1 does not carry it. The README's observation about Worlds opened by a Store is not a theorem composing these predicates for every allowed start. The second conservation clause also disappears for a denomination whenever any Turn's createdIds contains its byte representation. The stated exception does not identify a well creation or its permitted floor effect. A stronger value contract should state the local issuance rule and derive the aggregate invariant from it.

The minting floor exercises the balance-sum clause. It does not exercise the floor-sum clause, delegation caps, or theft resistance. Its end World being unreachable under deployed admission is correct for a mutation counterexample. That unreachability is not a vacuity defect. The deployed issue/account/transfer run now exercises actual value movement. It does not convert conservation into authority confinement.

Integrity.law is weaker than the promised history of the object's actual execution. The quantified log l occurs in Audited S w l w'. The returned ObjectHistory S.X obj w w' tr has no l parameter. It asks for some history between the same endpoints. ObjectHistory's constructors call the concrete receive and World.admit functions. They do not call S.receive and S.admit. The claim therefore mixes a variable implementation in its premise with an implementation-shaped witness in its conclusion. The proof constructs the witness by following the log. The statement does not retain that correspondence. Reviewers should not have to read the proof to recover the property the theorem is supposed to state.

The law-chain clause has another restriction beyond NoRef. A selected law must be present at every recorded step of the chosen tr. Its package must read as the same p in every such view. A law active during only part of the history does not receive this whole-history chain conclusion. The initial state must also decode at p.state. Objects created inside l are outside the direct quantification over objects live at w. Applying the theorem to suffixes after birth requires a further composition. The independent meaning of each old and incoming law at each actual replacement step should be stated directly.

A system that refuses every operation on an object can satisfy B3. A contextual evaluator that enforces the NoRef fragment while giving hole-bearing terms the wrong meaning is not excluded by the NoRef chain clause. ObjectHistory's operational judgments do not repair that semantic omission because they refer back to the same program implementation. The missing guarantee is a relation between the actual accepted operation and the contextual reference meaning under its actual pinned dependencies. NoRef is a supported-language restriction. It is not an environmental assumption that deployment machinery can discharge for a contextual program.

No top-level language statement here specifies metering. The chain conclusion contains no budget or charged-cost relation. It does not establish adequacy for a terminating reference computation with sufficient fuel. It does not state agreement of successful executions across sufficient fuel choices. It does not expose the semantic meaning of returned calls and relation edits as a property of the audited log. These omissions matter to the advertised metered contextual language. A program that always exhausts its budget is compatible with a one-way law-soundness claim. A general adequacy theorem would distinguish permitted resource refusal from an evaluator that refuses everything.

B3's floor changes a data object's package slot. The floor instantiates the law premises with a separate constant-true law. That law need not be installed on the object for the package conjunct to yield the contradiction. The floor therefore does not establish sensitivity to a skipped law check. The decisions file records this limitation explicitly. It also records the absence of a deployed law-judged witness. A kernel that preserves packages while bypassing a rejecting law is the countermodel the author's promise needs to exclude. Binding the object trace to l makes that countermodel a direct question about the actual step.

Consent.okCommitted proves that a call in a phase-4 continuation already has a journal entry in f.world. The member wants success to name the call authorized by its command. The predicate never relates argv to the decoded call c. The continuation's last accumulator element chooses c. A fabricated continuation carrying a previously committed unrelated call satisfies the success condition. No premise connects this continuation to Command.start or to the command's planning phase.

The conclusion also examines f.world rather than f'.world. A replacement follower can return success after erasing the journal from its resulting World. C(ii) still holds when the old World contained the supplied call. The current deployed phase-4 arm preserves the World. The top statement fails to preserve that fact. The hostWord floor catches an ok response for a call absent from the old journal. It does not catch wrong-command success or destructive success. inhabited_okCommitted runs an isolated settle-phase continuation on a prepared World. It does not establish a complete command execution from the member's argv.

Consent.final establishes a useful branch-local negative fact. After a final non-stale settlement, the same call cannot be accepted by submit on an extension of that World. The RefusalsCanon and JournalCanon premises support that conclusion. Deployment must establish them across its real starts and recovery edges. The theorem does not establish their preservation across crashes because crashes are not in its execution relation.

The conclusion does not say the call was never admitted earlier. It does not rule out admission on a sibling fork. It does not rule out another call representing the same user intent. It also does not characterize why the selected refusal code is true. An implementation that refuses every submit satisfies this negative property regardless of the quality of its settlement diagnoses. An implementation that never returns a final settlement satisfies it vacuously. The alwaysFinal floor excludes false finality for one call that the deployed submit accepts immediately. The expired witness reaches one substantive case. Neither artifact establishes correspondence between printed command outcomes and user intent.

Consent.cancelOk relates successful cancellation to S.settle on the resulting World. It does not independently state that a cancellation record exists. A follower that reports successful cancellation paired with a settlement function that always says cancelled satisfies this predicate. That countermodel fails Consent.final if submit still admits calls. It passes both predicates if submit rejects everything. The hostWord floor fixes settle to its deployed implementation, so it detects only the first component of this coupling. A meaningful cancellation theorem must connect the requested call, the authorized cancellation, its accepted record, and subsequent non-admission in the same retained history. No deployed successful cancellation witness appears in Floor.lean.

Consent.staleFinal is a conditional theorem about a call planned on an ancestor World. CellsFit, RowsCanon, RowsOk, signedReads, plan, and the two folds do the substantive work. RefusalsCanon and JournalCanon handle the recorded-refusal case. The explicit pin-input collision alternative handles the cryptographic exception. The claim never proves that the real command carried the same planned call through its continuation and eventual printed verdict. Its quantified cmd and body b are not tied by a body-from-command relation. The floor demonstrates that separation by using signedReads for rotate while planning issue 1000. This does not invalidate the finality proof. It prevents reading the theorem as a complete consent theorem.

Stale finality is also confined to extensions of the same branch. Another call for the same intent remains outside it. An always-open settlement makes the stale premise empty. The staleOnWord floor correctly catches an unjustified stale verdict at the planning World. It does not inhabit a deployed stale verdict reached after a real command's observation became stale. Removing the relay from the trust boundary requires an intent-to-continuation invariant, not just this OCC lemma.

Shown.onlyFollowers is an authorization condition on the defer constructor. It is not a confidentiality theorem. A handle that returns the World's serialized contents in Answer.reply satisfies it because it never defers. A handle can retain the deployed authorized defer behavior and leak the World through a different reply arm. That second countermodel also admits the existing inhabited_shown witness. The checked envelope is therefore insufficient to characterize all observations available to a non-member.

The openHost floor tests bypass of the particular defer guard. It does not test disclosure through other outputs. The claimed sentence that a non-member learns nothing requires an observational relation over all responses from an honest host. Permitted disclosures must be named in that relation. The current Host.handle also contains lookup responses that expose journal status without going through Envelope.check. The predicate does not address those responses. The charter trusts the operator for confidentiality. A theorem over the honest handler cannot prohibit an adversarial operator from releasing bytes it already possesses. The design's adversarial-writer prose must not use G2 to promise that prohibition.

The floor method proves discrimination against selected implementations. It does not prove adequacy of a specification. Refuting one mutant establishes that the predicate is not true of every System. It does not establish that every advertised conjunct constrains behavior. Refuting package mutation says nothing about the non-vacuity of the reference-law implication. Executing a rotate says nothing about contextual law execution. Establishing one successful settle phase says nothing about command provenance. These are consequences of the quantifiers, not requests for six thousand new plants.

Keep top-level mutation checks as diagnostic examples. Choose them from the failures members need excluded. Examples include a valid-looking unauthorized transfer, an unrelated-call success, rollback after success, a skipped rejecting law, and a fabricated foreign head. Establish supported behavior through general completeness or adequacy results where the contract requires it. The charter's exclusion of liveness proofs does not forbid proving that a valid page from an honest writer is accepted by the follower. That is a finite input-output agreement property. It also does not excuse an evaluator without semantic adequacy. The deployed useful-case witnesses should accompany those contracts without being mistaken for them.

The System record is a sound parameterization mechanism. It does not supply an independent specification. Audited means that the supplied kernel agrees with its own decisions. ObjectHistory embeds the concrete receiver again. Shown delegates authorization meaning to Envelope.check. Merely collecting those predicates in one directory moves much of the review boundary into their transitive definitions. A useful abstraction can be a relation over observations and authority effects. It does not need to be a second executable kernel. The design's claim that an independent model violates the rule against duplicate implementations confuses a specification with a competing implementation.

The record also leaves its fields semantically independent. Changing S.receive does not rewire the deployed follow closure to call the changed receiver. Changing S.admit does not rewire S.submit. This is acceptable for component countermodels. It is insufficient as a semantics of a composed replacement system. A whole-system theorem needs the actual callers and effects that connect these functions. Spec/System.lean contains no Command.start field, Store interpreter, crash transition, resident tick, broker, or authority-based receipt verifier. Its top theorems cannot establish properties of omitted executions.

SPEC-1 already records the deployment-wiring gap. The binaries do not obtain their behavior from a proved interpretation of this record. A binary wired to another function leaves the record's theorem intact. The remedy is an execution relation tied to the actual entry points and their effects. Compiler, FFI, custody, filesystem, and transport assumptions then belong at explicit boundaries of that relation. Proof checking can make proof scripts untrusted. It cannot make the meanings of imported predicates unreviewed. The design's promise that any meaning change beneath a claim becomes a build error is false. A weakened definition can make an old proof continue to succeed.

Authority confinement is missing from the landed apex. Conservation permits theft. Law enforcement permits every action admitted by an overly permissive law. Re-execution reproduces an authority bug on every member. The design's proposed B1 also needs revision before landing. Its endpoint implication asks for one authorized step when a cell differs between the beginning and end. That does not state that every intervening mutation was authorized. It ignores a sequence that changes a cell and restores it. It ignores deletion when no final live cell exists. The required statement ranges over every actual effect, including creation, retirement, delegation, nested invocation, debit, and key change. Its authority relation must be defined from the policy before that effect. Authority amplification and spending limits need explicit clauses.

Durability is missing from the listed predicates. A writer that acknowledges before persistence leaves these pure-function theorems unchanged. A follower that returns success before keeping its accepted prefix also leaves them unchanged. The deployment needs a trace property connecting acknowledgments to durable records under a named crash model. Recovery must preserve the acknowledged prefix and its audit provenance. The recovered remainder may include durable operations whose responses were lost. A correct contract must not reject that permitted case. Client attempts need an explicit intent identifier because repeated argv alone does not distinguish a retry from a deliberate second purchase.

The Bell is missing as an end-to-end statement. Its specification must follow the same ring identity through World state, resident intent, external ask, kept answer, and restart. The safety obligations are at most one charged ask per ring, at most one accepted answer, and correspondence between the accepted answer and the durable kept result or named restart outcome. The broker cap must survive those restarts. The trace must specify what counts as an ask at the provider boundary. A local counter cannot establish that property when a request was sent but its reply was lost. These obligations need no promise that the provider eventually responds.

Receipts between Worlds are missing from Spec. Protocol/Proofs/Foreign.lean exposes hA, a fold from genesis, and hr, equality of the held roots to that fold's roots. Those premises supply the connection to an actual source history. The receiving World's receipt path cannot manufacture that connection from a posted digest. The existing hA is a World.fold premise. It is not an Audited premise. Its conclusion establishes admission of a Turn in that fold. A claim about authorized source calls needs the additional audit and authority connection.

The current tree already distinguishes member attestations from authority certificates. Protocol.Head.certified checks a positive threshold of distinct listed certifier indices. Head.certifiesWorld checks the certificate against the adopted World's head when its authority has a positive threshold. Protocol/Proofs/Head.lean states intersection and equivocation results for conflicting certified chains at one step count. The adoption arm now consumes that certificate check. Spec.Start retains none of this evidence. Protocol.Foreign.check still checks receipt paths against the head object's supplied roots. It does not invoke the certificate verifier. The current certificate code describes certificate production as undeployed. Authority.none supplies no certifier-backed guarantee.

The design's proposed discharge of hr is wrong. A writer can sign exactly one fabricated head and satisfy the rule that it signs at most one head per step count. The fabricated head need not belong to any lawful fold. It creates no pair of conflicting signatures. A consistency link between fabricated heads does not supply the omitted lawful execution. Signature verification establishes attributable endorsement. It does not establish the endorsed proposition. The foreign-head contract must choose its source of validity explicitly: re-execution, an authenticated checkpoint trusted for validity, or an authority assumed to certify only valid extensions. A certificate alone supplies accountability evidence under its authority policy. [Certificate Transparency's treatment of misbehaving logs](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9162.html#section-11.3) likewise separates signed observations from the comparison needed to expose inconsistent behavior.

The authority's origin must itself be authenticated. Reading an authority from the untrusted object being checked would let the object appoint its own verifier. The current certifiesWorld comment correctly relies on prior trust in the pinned World bytes. A cross-World consumer needs an equivalent trusted binding of origin to authority. Quorum intersection establishes a common signing index. Interpreting that index as a culpable party additionally needs authenticated identities and signature unforgeability. Guaranteeing unique valid histories requires a behavioral assumption about honest certifiers. None of those facts follows from a threshold number alone.

ONE HISTORY ∧ INTEGRITY is not the right apex as currently stated. A member needs durable, authorized execution of its own intents with an explicit account of what other members and other Worlds can infer. The apex should be observational trace refinement to that contract. Under an honest writer, the contract supplies one authorized sequential history and durable acknowledgments. Under an adversarial writer, the contract supplies retained audited member views and a precisely stated fork-consistency guarantee. Authority-backed evidence supplies attribution at comparison points. Isolation without a comparison does not imply detected equivocation. The model must expose these trust cases rather than naming all of them one history.

The contract should permit delay and refusal under the charter's liveness scope. It should never permit invented consent, forgotten success, unauthorized effects, or unearned certainty about a foreign head. The Bell should instantiate this contract over its ring lifecycle. The metered language should supply the independent meaning of object transitions within it. The three required statements follow.

For an honest writer and the declared platform assumptions, every observable execution of the deployed member, Store, and resident processes refines the intent-indexed durable contract, including the Bell's per-ring charge and answer limits across retries and crashes.

Every accepted step implements an independently defined transition relation that confines authority, conserves value under the stated issuance rule, enforces delegation caps, and gives contextual programs and applicable laws their reference meaning within the declared resource budget.

Every accepted member view or foreign receipt is justified by a history extending its explicitly trusted start, with prefix consistency or checkable equivocation evidence under the declared head-authority and comparison assumptions.

I would delete Shown.onlyFollowers from the top-level contract and retain its theorem as an internal handler lemma.
