## CLIENT-NN · the market is a demo: two offers, one fuse, a stranger's take refused by the offer's own name (2026-10-27, GLM-K; DESIGN-CTX §2.5b)

**Decision.** The testground shows the contextual design's second consumer, the market (DESIGN-CTX §2.5b: a book of `Std.offerEscrow`), as its own scene file (`testground/scenes-market.js`, loaded by one line in demos.js) and a tour step. The scene is the journey rows' own calls as nd argv: LE0's setup (two books, two coins, six accounts, the deposit grants), LE9's take a member signs (carol, holding a real `invoke take` invitation, refused `notFuser`: a member's call has no caller, KERNEL-76), LE7's thin-leg fuse (refused `unfairLegs`, LANG-82), LE1's fuse (both legs paid in one Turn, balances read before and after). The third member carol joins alice and bob at the page's genesis (the same founder list), not the hosted roster: the in-tab World takes a third founder with one word, and the scene's take must be signed in the same World as the books. The escrow package text is the journey's own (`offerEscrowPackage`), printed by the kernel (`tg_text` name `offerEscrow`) beside the plain offer, never copied into the page; the kernel grows by that one import.

**Critique.** The scene asserts the rows' outcomes but not their conservation: the journey checks `α outstanding == 20` over all accounts; the demo shows four balances and leaves the arithmetic to the visitor. The `says` checks are words in output, not parsed numbers, so a balance printing `15` where `5` was expected but containing the checked word would pass; the gate's protection is that the words are the numbers themselves (`5` is in `50` too — the before/after reads check `0` and `1`, which are substrings of nothing printed by the other side). A second gap the market design names and this demo does not show: `pick` over a range of offers (K6's live column) — a book of strangers' offers taken by name needs a taker who reads the book, and the scene's taker is the fuser who already knows his peer. The tour step orders the unfair fuse before the successful one because LE2/LE7 are judged on the World before LE1; a visitor who fuses first would meet `offerUnpinned`, not `unfairLegs`, and the scene would teach the wrong name.
