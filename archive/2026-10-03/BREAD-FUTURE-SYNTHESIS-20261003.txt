DECISION FRAME

Mini should inherit Bread's diversity of things people can actually do, and improve the common mechanisms that make those things coexist. Bread is neither merely a predecessor kernel nor a feature checklist. Its corpus contains several distinct product lineages: collective fiction and social worlds; games and authored universes; private economic coordination; agent homes and public utilities; a programmable desktop/docuverse; participant-operated cloud services; and independent verification. A good synthesis has to preserve these on their own terms before choosing their shared machinery.

The initial inspector-centered hypothesis was too narrow as an organizing account of all Bread. Inspectors remain a valuable connection, but a fiction engine does not become valuable merely by being inspectable, a social world is not a work dashboard, and private resource coordination is not just another document action. Coherence means one actual identity/authority/state/history where the applications interact, with suitable different experiences; it does not require one genre, screen, or generic workflow engine.

FIVE STRONGEST CREATIVE BETS

• A community-authored station: rooms, instruments, games, scenes and collective decisions are extensible resources, with inhabitants able to author new pieces. Neither a fixed dungeon nor an office dashboard defines the substrate.
• A living research/work desk: documents, exact source excerpts, computations, model work and disagreements remain connected and navigable; claims carry their actual evidence regime. Reusing the same objects as personal tools and shared artifacts makes the world teachable.
• A workshop commons: members publish useful recipes, offer bounded services/capacity, and privately coordinate work through explicit contracts. The first public/native route and later private route share semantics; no disconnected market ledger.
• Rehearsal as a normal act: try a world/method/doc change in a confined branch, keep competing interpretations visible, then incorporate a chosen result through current native law. This connects fiction, software and research without pretending all conflicts commute.
• Portable places and instruments: people and agents can retain work, narrow a shared view, recover at another host and keep acknowledged history. The mature seed is not a vague portable agent: Bread has renter-signed acknowledged prefixes, exact package/body identity and typed one-generation-ahead archive recovery. Technical embodiment/native/mobile and stronger custody are later expansions of this product, not discarded aspirations.

My priority is the community-authored station and living research/work desk as two different consumers of the same completed native contracts; then workshop commons and accountable portability as substantive economic/distributed extensions. Rehearsal belongs across those experiences, with explicit limits until lawful installation and physical-effect confinement exist. This ranking is my recommendation, not a recovered human vote among genres. None requires replacing an already useful plain service interface with game fiction.

WHAT WOULD MAKE MINI MORE CAPABLE AND SIMPLER

Use existing native objects and receipts to join these experiences. A few shared contracts are worth completing: source-pinned definitions/constructors; independently authorized observations and views; exact invocation/effect results; explicit asynchronous work identities/artifacts; and membership/rule-change/recovery obligations. These are connections to Mini's current contracts, not a request for another universal schema or semantic bus.

Delete duplication through adoption: no per-application private executor presented as a shared world, no UI-only authority, no separate app identity shadow, no provider result promoted to semantic truth, no local rollback presented as distributed atomicity, no grant recreated by reopening an old screenshot, and no unbounded global scan behind every interaction. Preserve distinctions that carry meaning: story canon versus factual evidence, pure computation versus serviced effects, append-only history versus mutable state, proposal versus commitment, transport admission versus business settlement, historical validity versus present authorization.

The smallest decisive receiving scenario should exercise several of these joins in the SAME Mini world, then a second genuinely different consumer should refute accidental specialization. Example: a member authors a new room/scene behavior, another participant acts through a different view, a resident reads independently authorized source material and proposes a change, a collective/private method decides one exact effect, and a crash/reconnect recovers the same work/result without a second charge. A separate useful workshop or research tool uses the same contracts with different content/behavior. Two examples are evidence of extensibility, not a fixed two-person platform limit.

COUNTERFACTUAL REPLAY — RECONSTRUCT THE DESIGN EXPERIMENT, NOT A MYTH OF INEVITABILITY

Ember's new direction is to replay the creation of dregg from actual prompts, attempted constructions, failures and research, using what is now known to design a coherent fresh system. That is a different activity from replaying the runtime log. It is also different from a future Git-history splice or moving Mini into Bread as a subdirectory. No repository rewrite, merge, migration or universal port is authorized by this synthesis.

For each replay unit, retain five separate records: (1) dated human intent and subsequent correction; (2) the attempted design and its source identity; (3) actual result and failure evidence, including which consumer ran; (4) what later work taught us; (5) the proposed alternative today. Keep inferred causal rationale labeled as inference. A chronological prompt catalog alone is insufficient, and hindsight must not masquerade as what the original author knew.

FINAL TRANSFER DECISIONS AND ADVERSARIAL PRIORITIES

The best immediately reusable code is comparatively small; the richest inheritance is often a mature contract plus its consumer and failure cases. These are the most consequential concrete decisions from the joined reports:

1. Extract pure, bounded presentation data when the current receiving consumer needs it. deos-view's ViewNode/mount limits and selected text/web lowering are better code donors than the native desktop crate. Keep Mini signed authorized projections and current native actions as its inputs. Cost: low-to-moderate adapter work, moderate consistent user-input/event semantics; default GPUI/SpiderMonkey dependencies and backend input mismatch make wholesale crate adoption expensive. Inspectors should connect notebook/tool/story/work experiences, not define all of them.

2. Carry authored content and validated edit/publication contracts into the existing Mini story/tool path. spween grammar, shared-draft typed edits, UGC author+parent+content binding, and saga's same-completion/identity joins are substantial.
