WHY THIS AGREEMENT CONSTRUCTION, AND WHAT WOULD CHANGE IT
October 3, 2026 — Astra, agreement-engine owner
Preservation of research and design rationale; not a claim that the destination is implemented.

The decision is to build complete Generic Simplex 1/3 view agreement as one concrete, source-owned local agreement engine, while preserving independent governance and operation-specific joint turns above it. It is not a conclusion that Simplex is the best consensus protocol in general, that a single total order defines Mini, or that the comparative research is exhausted. The most important alternative is to reuse the agreement already required inside robust private computation when it can also select a unique, recoverable private successor. Censorship-resistant Prefix Consensus, asynchronous DAG hybrids, Bread's scoped blocklace, and coded dissemination each retain advantages that this first engine does not provide.

This document distinguishes paper results, our design inferences, source constructions and actual execution evidence. Older architecture reports are preserved below but contain superseded decisions. In particular: the joint final-signer refinement supersedes symmetric early YES as the preferred default; q durable original COMMIT SEND signatures supersede q local doCommit outputs for transferable engine certificates; the five-wave complete-instance cost supersedes the early two-wave estimate; and a physical allocation receipt is not qualified private successor custody.

THE DESIRED COMPUTER

The object realm should allow people and agents to inhabit independently governed worlds, run general methods, delegate authority, retain private state, and cooperate through explicitly authorized joint actions. Two objects may share a compute cohort without sharing a lawgiver. Conversely, one operation may require several laws even when one machine evaluates it. A world is not merely a named chain, and a reference from one object to another must not silently enroll both committees into one electorate.

This is grounded in user intent, not inferred from the easiest protocol to implement. WHAT HAS TO AGREE, AND WHAT AGREEMENT CANNOT DO

There are at least six separate receiving obligations:

1. Delivery: the bytes/evidence needed by the next actor actually arrive and remain obtainable under the specified failures. A hash or signature does not provide its preimage.
2. Ordering/decision: conflicting accepted choices have a monotone common interpretation. A sufficient certificate may exist even when no one honest actor has yet assembled it.
3. Validity/admission: the actual source interpreter authorizes the exact command at the exact predecessor, including authority, clocks, budgets, aliases, negative reads, returned values and effects. An operator's Boolean or independently invented validator is insufficient.
4. Installation: one actual atomic source-store operation installs the transition, nullifiers, receipt and required outbox; uncertainty after a lost physical reply is reconciled rather than guessed away.
5. Custody: future execution can reconstruct the exact successor material or coherent secret-sharing generation, without the vanished submitting client. Equal public output hashes do not prove coherent shares.
6. Release: current audience authority permits the particular observation. A finalized secret may remain private and become another computation's input. Physical effects require idempotency/reconciliation beyond consensus.

This separation is constructive. Reaching Univalency defines an extractor over arbitrary authenticated transcript subsets: nonempty extracted answers agree, although no individual is promised all the evidence. It therefore gives a cheaper solved task, not a free substitute for all-recipient delivery or a usable private execution receipt [P2]. Carnot turns the first correct reconstruction into a specified subsequent dissemination obligation [P3]. Our inference is to retain distinct typed predicates and join them in the native receiver, rather than make one overloaded 'commit' bit stand for them all.

Bread already has important parts of this shape. Its JointTurn/JointFamily theorems require explicit cross-leg binding, and scoped ReferenceGroup ordering and cross-reference kinds separate governance from shared causal transport. The executor's bilateral schedule binds both sides of transfers/grants/introductions. Its source-bound durable promise outbox is a reusable physical pattern. These are positive mechanisms, not just caveats. The October3 source audit at Bread22fad151e9f118e3aa001a823e075918ffa139e8 is [A3]. It also explains why pure jointApply, receipt import, and a test-only coordinator do not establish Byzantine atomic completion across sovereign private domains. No current Bread runtime re-audit was performed for this preservation document.

WHY COMPLETE GENERIC SIMPLEX IS THE PRESENT LOCAL BASELINE

The selected algorithm is arXiv2609.32985v1, September26: outer protocol Figure1 and 1/3-VA Figures4/5, with the full continuation/timeout rules and AppendixC, not a two-message fast-path approximation [P1]. It has an explicit chain-valued interface, separates preparation from unique commitment, and continues old view work while later views progress. A safe proposal extends prepared ancestry and proves intervening views disabled. Commit excludes conflicting preparation and disabling in that view. Those mechanisms match a durable local source-history service and are comparatively direct to connect to executable Lean state transitions.

Its coin-free, signature-free abstract communication is useful under the PQ preference: pairwise authentication can be realized separately from public certificate export, without assuming compact PQ threshold signatures or a trusted coin service. That is a practical construction advantage, not a quantum-UC or adaptive/mobile theorem. The current native experiment actually uses ML-DSA65 authenticated messages/certificate material; it does not receive the paper's signature-free byte price automatically.

The theorem model is fixed membership, static Byzantine faults, n=3f+1 and q=2f+1, authenticated reliable channels, unknown GST and a KNOWN delay bound Delta thereafter, with the paper's clock/delivery assumptions. Valid/extend are locally evaluable with negligible cost in the latency analysis. Table1 gives 3delta healthy commitment, 5delta eventual worst-case commitment with active Byzantine behavior, and 2delta failure-free proposal interval; configured timeout is at least7Delta for the selected version. These are different endpoints from client-to-installed-private-result latency.

Our actual source validity is expensive and may be unavailable. A concrete liveness counterexample during construction had q supporting messages for x but a correct replica never obtaining checked(x) at the exact parent; fair transport alone could not make it prepare. Consequently the native participant must disseminate candidates, schedule refetch/checking fairly and reproduce the exact historical source prefix. Rechecking x against today's unrelated source tip is wrong. The implemented historical validator reconstructs the canonical source records from the pinned origin and uses actual NativeHostReplay; only its opaque accepted value enables Input.checked. This is our implementation join and additional progress premise, not supplied by naming the paper's validity predicate [S2].

The cost correction is central to the selection's honesty. The literal healthy 1/3-VA instance continues VOTE, CANDIDATE, COMMIT and both READY rules: five all-to-all full-value waves plus leader dissemination. With a hypothetical4160-byte value-plus-header, [5n(n-1)+(n-1)]*4160 is5,054,400bytes at n16. It excludes repair, ingress, persistence, actual authentication, private evaluation and cover traffic. Removing duplicate READY sends is a refinement requiring proof; stopping after local commit is not a substitute. Full history repeated in a value is especially expensive. A digest/custody adaptation is attractive but must preserve retrieval, ancestry validity and old obligations, not merely replace x by H(x) [P1,A1].

The source experiment also corrected an erroneous certificate assumption. One honest replica can locally doCommit after selective delivery while others have emitted COMMIT without locally committing. Demanding q local doCommit exports can then strand a valid decision. The native transferable witness now authenticates q distinct original durable COMMIT SEND events, bound to exact Context/view/full block. The receiver keeps the full descendant certificate and uses a certified source-prefix relation to append the next actual record; it never invents a signature over a truncated block. SourceApplied is a separate actual appended/read-back fact. The proof owner's AttributedCommitSends and ReceiverSafety target match that boundary [S1,S2].

This is why Simplex was selected for a first executable baseline: a fitting interface, a concrete complete algorithm, coin-free progress, and inspectable proof obligations. It was not chosen because independent governance needs one universal log, because three delays dominate every cost, or because existing dependencies were convenient.

JOINT TURNS WITHOUT AN UNRELATED SUPER-COMMITTEE

The joint candidate must bind exact canonical native command/effect bytes, required domains and their epochs, attempt and invocation lineage, complete semantic dependencies, each exact install projection, and custody-generation references. Digests accompany exact bytes; collisions or ambiguous encoding must not redefine identity. Per-domain q thresholds are checked against that domain's pinned roster. A majority across the union has no authority meaning when each local law is necessary.

Our design law is all required YES for the same candidate implies Commit; any required NO implies Abort. Within a domain, quorum intersection plus source slot single-assignment excludes both. Therefore simultaneous Commit and Abort would require a contradiction at the domain contributing NO. This simple argument relies on real scope completeness, non-equivocating durable local decisions and cross-candidate exclusivity; it does not prove those premises by itself.

The design-room refinement binds a required final signer d*, normally the mandatory invocation/nullifier origin. Others can finalize YES/NO; d* keeps its choice open until it retains all other YES plus the necessary bodies/custody, revalidates/reserves its own projection and atomically records its YES. Its earlier NO prevents a late remote YES from resurrecting commitment. This permits a live required origin to abort while another domain is silent. It trades away the symmetric scheme's possibility of completing later after the origin disappears. It is not universally nonblocking atomic commit, and d* is not an added unrelated coordinator quorum [A2].

Current authority needs an explicit promise commit point. Ordinary permission to execute now does not imply permission to reserve an irrevocable later installation. The receiver therefore uses purpose-specific source-law admission and protects its relevant read dependencies in the same source image. For private work, initial durable PENDING can lock/fund work and authorize physical allocation; terminal YES waits for actual qualifying custody evidence. A physical allocation receipt alone cannot establish a recoverable secret successor. Before YES, a source-governed sticky NO can release that local pending reservation; after YES, timeout alone cannot revoke it. The first general joint visibility additionally needs the required actual installations, not merely unanimous votes [S3,A2].

Recovery is an authorized durable continuation, potentially holding a hidden participant mask, exact candidate/generation, phase and evidence-access rights. Public certificates do not entitle strangers to learn a private dependency graph or recover secret material. The recovery job must have source funding and independently reserved service. A vanished caller cannot be the only holder of this responsibility; no coordinator callback can manufacture a missing domain's consent. These remain construction/proof obligations beyond the ordinary four-store path.

WHERE THE OTHER CONSTRUCTIONS REMAIN BETTER OR OPEN

Bread scoped blocklace / Constitutional Consensus. Bread offers causal multi-producer dissemination, group-scoped order and rich joint receipts that should be retained. The audit found its old growing-ratifier-union issue repaired by fixed anchor closures, so citing that historical bug as current would be wrong. It also found ChainExtends still a premise of the live order-extraction argument and old roster pinning preserving known crossed tallies rather than every still-possible certificate. Constitutional Consensus's immutable closure, endorsement/ratification and tau-prefix proof provide a positive repair template [P4,A3]. A native monotone extractor plus complete epoch liability transfer could make this a superior shared dissemination/decision engine. The present Simplex choice does not discard Bread's semantic mechanisms.

Flutter/Blink. The current Fast Byzantine Total Order Broadcast version supplies adaptive-fault, signature-free total-order broadcast at n=5f+1, direct client submission and an explicit eventual correct-client inclusion property. Future-time bets, authenticated FIFO observations and lock times close the set of earlier potentially accepted requests. Blink's fast decision still runs its underlying binary agreement afterward. Its2Delta+epsilon client-broadcast endpoint is attractive for sparse interactive scopes. Its bounded-clock/FIFO model, one-fifth resilience, retries, fallback traffic and candidate retention differ materially from Simplex. It could win if those premises fit the product; it is not merely an obsolete binary primitive [P5,A1].

Accountable Prefix Consensus. This supplies an actual censorship mechanism: low/high prefix evidence exposes truncating proposers and agreed rank demotion prevents the same Byzantine proposer omitting honest inputs indefinitely. Its multi-slot theorem bounds slots omitting honest proposer inputs by f after an effective stabilization time. That is stronger than Simplex's recurring honest-leader opportunities. It does not prove fair relative order or external-client admission. Its optimized certificates use threshold/BLS aggregation; substituting one small PQ signature is invalid. Basic ordinary-signature construction remains possible but expensive. A compact PQ accountability construction and proved pipeline would reopen selection for censorship-sensitive scopes [P6,A1].

Steelhead, Icarus, Ipotane and Frosty. These are positive transition mechanisms, not 'choose whichever path wins.' Steelhead retains each pending slot's own evidence window and uses a second DAG interpretation to choose adaptation pivots even while output stalls. Its formal reference-set model assumes production, propagation, pacing and non-anticipating coin conditions rather than proving the native message loop. Icarus aligns a certified-chain cutoff; Ipotane preserves fast commits while productive fallback runs concurrently. Frosty's fallback anchor carries stable preferences, not just locally finalized registers. Such constructions can outperform a simple known-Delta controller under bad leaders or uncertain timing, but need their coin, evidence and switching invariant instantiated under the same custody/privacy/PQ profile [P7,A4]. Frosty's analyzed population/security budget is not a ready theorem for four-node worlds.

Carnot, Multimmit and Pipes. Carnot's two-round lower bound is a cut-information theorem for its specified delivered-broadcast task, not a universal2.5x bound on every DAG or private storage layer. Carnot2 LemmaB.7 gives the stronger constructive lesson: a first correct decoder's checked recovery representation and late echoes spread the block and ancestry by max(t,GST)+2delta+s. Multimmit preserves independently disseminated producer chains and complete fresh prefix vectors through ordering, preventing a stale leader entry erasing useful work. Pipes explicitly prices finite upload/download service and metadata/payload dependency; streaming independent work changes bottlenecks without skipping validation. These are serious candidates to replace the prototype's repeated full-payload/history transfer. Re-encoding secret shares requires a separate private recovery construction; ciphertext reconstruction alone does not supply it [P3,P8,P9,A5].

Dependency agreement / transactions. Fast BFT SMR and Pesto show that payload/dependency agreement and certified concurrency can replace compulsory global total ordering for some semantics. Mini must compare returned values, authority, budgets, negative predicates, receipts and effects, not only final writes. A public conflict graph is a different privacy profile from hidden object relationships. The retained study found a missing complete upper-protocol proof in the read Fast BFT SMR edition and a Pesto query-quorum discrepancy needing resolution; these are research work, not reasons to pretend the constructions do not exist [A4].

Fused private agreement. Robust AMPC already selects inputs and performs agreement internally. Adding a separate public intake log for the same choice is unjustified. A further completion choice is also removable if the private protocol fixes one invocation/attempt/predecessor, canonical result, one authenticated recoverable successor sharing generation, unique reservation/install decision and retry lineage. Public output equality alone proves none of the secret-state continuity. FIN/index-ACS is a useful small-n asynchronous comparator, but its measured CKS coin is classical; 2024/677 ASKS/coin-free ACS plus native AVSS batching is a concrete PQ-oriented bootstrap/refill direction, not a completed substitution theorem for AMPC Facs/Fba. The newer2024/1710 adaptive hash-based MVBA/ACS has important stronger fault theorems but distinct-party sampling counts69/137 do not literally fit n4/10. Current source audits also found raw-share/error-correction and finite-coin-stock gaps in a candidate external implementation. Do not replace those gaps with an ideal coin and call the private machine finished [P10,A6].

LIFETIME, CAPACITY AND OBSERVATION ASSUMPTIONS

Fixed membership is a primitive premise, not a lifetime governance policy. Epoch close must transfer every installed state, YES/NO, pending install/custody/recovery obligation and still-possible old certificate liability. A successor roster cannot reinterpret an old certificate using today's threshold. A final semantic cut followed only by inert old-epoch descendants is a proposed way to keep late sufficient evidence comparable with close; the executable reconfiguration and retirement proof are not completed merely by pinning Context. Constitutional Consensus and the permissioned-to-PoS compiler offer specific handover mechanisms, while the recent DAG reconfiguration paper has explicit restrictions on changes, cumulative corruption and the final continuing configuration [P4,P11,A1].

Static/cumulative corruption must be stated honestly: 'at most f currently corrupt' does not protect an old epoch if the adversary later gathers enough retired signing keys. Forward security, erasure, proactive refresh and secret-share conversion require actual mechanisms and proofs. Likewise durable nonrollback storage is a security premise: restart with preserved votes is different from rolling back a signer. A crash may consume availability budget until recovery. No mobile/PQ-UC claim follows from using ML-DSA.

Known Delta must include serialization, reserved slot waiting, native verification and durable writes at admitted load. Finite fuel/queue/refusal bounds protect resources but do not constitute unbounded service liveness. A fair local scheduler is not a censorship law. Preprocessing refill, vote traffic, application data and repair need separate sustainable allocations; finite coin/pad stock does not prove indefinite progress. Portable streaming CAS removes the accidental whole-file16MiB limit but not whole-memory replay, growing full-block ancestry or network message bounds. Protected checkpoint/retirement remains necessary.

Traffic privacy must state its observer and permitted leakage. Constant shaped traffic can hide activity from packet observers while authorized plaintext Pending/staleness probes reveal dependency information. Common custody, issuer and law epochs are not interchangeable. Choosing a public capacity class, exposing participants or admitting failure leakage is a product/security decision, not a silent performance trick. The ordinary source prototype contains public canonical source records; it has not become a private-world transport merely because cryptography authenticates them [A2,A4].

WHAT WOULD REOPEN THE DECISION

A qualified small-cohort private ACS/coin/output construction that already selects usable coherent successors could remove the separate completion engine. A Bread/DAG execution refinement proving monotone extraction, availability and old-certificate closure could make shared dissemination cheaper. Compact PQ accountable-prefix evidence could justify stronger censorship guarantees. A deliberate one-fifth resilience and tight-clock/FIFO profile could favor Flutter. A coded Simplex refinement with first-decoder repair could materially reduce traffic without changing the semantic interface. Sustained churn/mobile compromise would invalidate baseline lifetime assumptions and require redesign rather than a larger timeout.

The decisive experiments are whole-operation comparisons: same source laws, complete dependencies, same payloads and usable custody, same admitted workload and traffic/repair budgets. Inject a withheld body, delayed sufficient old certificate, restarted signer, concurrent revoke, hidden alias, lost private share and lost physical reply. Observe exact invocation outcome, all required installations, retained future usability, worst sender queue and all traffic until old obligations drain. First local commit latency alone cannot choose the computer.
